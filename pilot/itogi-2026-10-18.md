# Summary as of October 18, 2026: what worked and what did not

All numbers are measured on Qwen2.5-14B unless stated otherwise. One model: this is the main limitation of the whole list.

## Worked (measured)

1. **The model has a habit, and we caught it.** The model learned a rule from examples, then got the note "the rule has changed" and still answered the old way:
   - 26 out of 100 without examples;
   - 74 out of 100 after 20 examples;
   - 82 out of 100 after 60 examples.

   Like a driver who has used the same road for years and drives past a new "detour" sign.

2. **This is mostly copying, not stubbornness.** If the history contains exactly the same case, the old answer is given in 93 out of 100. If the cases are similar but not identical, 38 out of 100, almost as with no history at all (37). The model copies the ready answer from the notebook.

3. **We found where the copying lives.** It is about 20 "heads" out of 1920 inside the model.
   - Switching them off halves the old answers.
   - Switching off 20 random heads changes nothing.
   - Silencing these heads only for the old examples does not hurt ordinary tests (MMLU, GSM8K).

4. **Inside the model there is an "about to copy" signal.** A small detector recognizes a habit error in advance with a quality of 0.91 out of 1 (AUC). It works only on our tasks.

5. **Where the habit appears and where it does not.**
   - On simple changes ("the word now means something else") the model is right in 98–100 out of 100 even with lots of old copies.
   - The habit shows up when part of a compound condition "A and at the same time B" changes.

   This is a finding about where the automatism lives.

6. **The honest testing method works.** We write the plan in advance, you name the number, and an independent reviewer recomputed everything from scratch: no tuning and no errors in the numbers.

## Did not work

1. **"Fu" training** (teaching the model not to copy). On our tasks it worked (86 → 0 old answers), but on independent tests it did not help and in places hurt: hindsight −29, GSM8K −6. The model learned our format, not the skill. The line is closed.

2. **The copying heads do nothing on public tests.** There the model pulls not from the examples in the text but from what it learned in training. This is a second automatism, which we have not touched yet.

3. **Someone else's "conflict lamp"** (from the paper by Zhao and co-authors). On its own facts it works perfectly, but it fires on everything, even on ordinary MMLU.

4. **The watchman has not yet proven its value.** Compared with the plain model it helps: on parcels 28 → 80 out of 100 without harm. But the reviewer showed that simply throwing away the whole history gives the same or more:
   - parcels: 90 vs 80 for the watchman;
   - bonus: 92 vs about 20.

   In our tasks the rules are written out in full, so the history is useless there.

5. **The plus on a public test is not real.** The rise 45 → 50 turned out to be just a shift towards one answer. Always giving that answer gets 55.

6. **The watchman is no smarter than the model itself.** If the model itself understands the new rule poorly (bonus), the watchman wrongly rejects good examples, and the result is harm of −7.

## Not tested

- Other models. The script is ready, the technical check on Qwen 7B and OLMo-2 13B passed, the run is paused.
- Tasks where history is needed: part of the rule is visible only from the examples. Only there can the idea "vote = sameness of conditions" be tested honestly.
- The pull towards knowledge from training (the second automatism).

## In short

We found the automatism itself, where it sits inside the model, and a signal that it is about to fire. This is a real result, but so far on one model and our own tasks. We do not yet have a fix that is better than a simple "forget the history" and works on independent tasks.
